# Evidence Validation

Read when researching or correcting external claims. Consistent documents can still
share an unsupported claim; cross-document agreement is not evidence.

## Ground the claims that affect decisions

1. List the consequential claims and their dependent decisions: capability, support,
   pricing, market size, regulatory duty, or a technical limitation.
2. Inspect supplied evidence. Verify external facts that may have changed against
   current sources; use primary documentation for technical claims, original datasets
   or research for quantitative claims, and official authorities for legal claims.
   Match the relevant version, product variant, geography, and date.
3. Record a source link, title, publication/version date when available, access date,
   the supported claim, and limitations. Link stored project evidence where relevant.
   Ordinary Markdown citations are sufficient; no separate citation tool is required.
4. Distinguish source statements, inference, and proposed assumptions. A source can
   support a feature without proving the user's demand, economics, or chosen design.
5. Correct the canonical claim, then its dependent product, operations, finance, and
   validation plans. Record the validation date for the claims actually checked.

For a group of claims, a research note can use:

| Claim | Source and location | Version/date | Finding | Implication or unresolved limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Keep extracts brief and cite them. Do not copy full external documents merely to
populate the blueprint. Evidence limitations stay visible; if a source is unavailable,
leave the claim unvalidated and record the missing evidence instead of inventing it.

## Promotion rules

- A user choosing an approach establishes a decision, not independent validation.
- An interview supports what that participant reported; distinguish sample findings
  from market-wide conclusions and record who was sampled without exposing identities.
- An estimate or simulation supports a conditional result, not a real-world outcome.
- A document with important unresolved hypotheses remains `[DRAFT]`; identify any
  validated claims individually and explain what still needs testing.
- A change in version, context, or premise can require rechecking previously validated
  evidence. Preserve provenance while updating the current interpretation.

Prioritize research that could change the decision. A scaffold may document a
validation method without conducting a full research project unless requested.
